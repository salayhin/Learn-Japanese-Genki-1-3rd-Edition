# Japanese / Genki I Resources

## Knowledge

### Primary texts

> **All page references in this workspace are the numbers printed in the books**, so they
> work with any copy. No file links anywhere — see the copyright note in [[NOTES.md]].

- **GENKI I: An Integrated Course in Elementary Japanese, 3rd Ed.** (Banno, Ikeda,
  Ohno, Shinagawa, Tokashiki — The Japan Times, March 2020, ISBN 978-4-7890-1730-5)
  The spine of the whole course. Two halves, numbered continuously:
  会話・文法編 (Conversation & Grammar, pp. 30–293) and 読み書き編 (Reading & Writing,
  from p. 296). Use for: everything. The 読み書き編 half is the reading-fluency engine
  and is the half most self-learners skip.

- **GENKI I Workbook, 3rd Ed.** (same authors, ISBN 978-4-7890-1731-2, March 2020)
  One worksheet per grammar point, numbered to match the textbook's grammar numbering,
  so sheets slot directly into the session plan. Structure verified against its own
  table of contents:
  - 会話・文法編 — Writing System cards (pp. 11, 23), Greetings (13), Numbers (15),
    then Lessons 1–12 (pp. 16–118). Each lesson ends with 答えましょう (written
    questions) and 聞く練習 (listening, 3–4 tasks per lesson).
  - 読み書き編 — **hiragana practice sheets pp. 121–127** (7 sheets, incl. one for
    dots/circles/small やゆよ and one for double consonants and long vowels),
    **katakana sheets pp. 128–132** (5 sheets), then Kanji Practice + Using Kanji,
    two pages per lesson, pp. 133–152.
  - The 読み書き編 kana sheets are handwriting practice. Given that handwriting is
    explicitly out of scope in [[MISSION.md]], use them as *reading* material and
    tracing warm-ups, not as a writing course.

- [**Genki-Online — official Self-Study Room**](https://genki3.japantimes.co.jp/en/student/)
  The publisher's own free hub: supplementary materials and linked Quizlet sets.
  Use for: legitimate extra drills, and the authoritative kanji/vocab lists.

- [**OTO Navi** (free audio app, iOS/Android)](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/oto-navi-sound-navigator/id1490391845)
  The official Genki audio, free — search the book's ISBN in-app. Has 3-second
  rewind and speed control, both of which matter for repeated reading.
  Use for: every 読み書き passage and every dialogue. **Non-optional for this
  mission** — reading fluency built without an audio model builds in bad decoding.

### Kana (the current bottleneck)

- [**Tofugu — Learn Hiragana: Ultimate Guide**](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese/learn-hiragana/)
  and [**Learn Katakana: Ultimate Guide**](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese/learn-katakana/)
  The best free mnemonic-based kana guides in existence. Mnemonics are the
  right tool here because kana are arbitrary — nothing about ア signals "a", so
  an artificial hook beats repetition. Use for: the katakana you don't yet know.
  Tofugu also publish free downloadable versions of both guides, linked from those pages.

- [**Kana Pro**](https://kana.pro/) — browser + iOS, free tier.
  Timed kana quizzing with per-character weak-point tracking. Use for: the daily
  5-minute speed drill once accuracy is there. Timer on.

- [**KanaQuiz** (Amherst)](https://wtawa.people.amherst.edu/kanaquiz/instructions.html)
  No account, no app, runs anywhere. Use for: a fallback drill when offline apps
  are inconvenient.

### Reading practice

- [**Tadoku free graded readers**](https://tadoku.org/japanese/en/free-books-en/)
  Free, levelled, CC-licensed. Level 0 is under 400 words and aimed at pre-N5.
  Use for: the "read something that isn't a textbook" milestone from Week 6 on.
- [**NHK News Web Easy**](https://www3.nhk.or.jp/news/easy/)
  Real news, simplified, with furigana and audio. Use for: after Genki L6 — it is
  above level before that and will only discourage.

### Method (why the plan is shaped the way it is)

- [**Gorsuch, Taguchi & Umehara (2015), "Repeated Reading for Japanese Language
  Learners," *The Reading Matrix* 15(2)**](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1075950)
  Semester-long study: re-reading the *same* passage several times with an audio
  model raised reading speed and comprehension, and the gain transferred to
  *unseen* passages. This is the evidence behind the 5-pass reading protocol in
  the roadmap.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [**r/LearnJapanese**](https://www.reddit.com/r/LearnJapanese/)
  The main English-language hub; the wiki's "Getting Started" guide is genuinely
  good and heavily moderated against bad advice. Use for: "is my study plan sane?",
  stuck-on-a-grammar-point questions, and the weekly beginner question threads —
  post there rather than making a top-level thread.
- [**WaniKani Community forums**](https://community.wanikani.com/)
  Slower, kinder, more beginner-tolerant than Reddit, and there are long-running
  Genki study threads. Use for: accountability, and the book-club threads when
  you reach Tadoku readers.
- [**HelloTalk**](https://www.hellotalk.com/) — language exchange with native speakers.
  Use for: sanity-checking that what you can read, you can also be understood
  saying. Worth starting only around Week 5, once you have L1–L3 grammar.

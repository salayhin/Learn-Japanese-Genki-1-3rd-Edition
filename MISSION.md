# Mission: Japanese with Genki I (3rd Edition)

## Why

To function in Japan in writing — read the signs, menus, station boards and forms
that surround daily life there — with an eye on relocating for work and on
certifying that ability with JLPT N5/N4. The bottleneck is reading: spoken
survival phrases can be memorised, but a train platform sign cannot be guessed.
Reading is therefore the spine of this mission, not a side effect of it.

## Success looks like

- Reading all 104 kana (hiragana + katakana, incl. dakuten and yōon) by sight,
  under **1.0 second per character**, with no sounding-out.
- Reading a katakana travel word cold — `スーツケース`, `チェックイン`, `エレベーター` —
  and knowing what it says before finishing it.
- Reading a Genki 読み書き passage from a lesson already studied, aloud, unseen,
  with comprehension and without a dictionary.
- Holding the grammar to build and understand a sentence in past, negative and
  て-form — the core of N5.
- Finishing able to open a Tadoku Level 0 graded reader and get through it.

## Constraints

- **45–50 minutes, weekdays only. Nothing on weekends.** This is a hard boundary,
  not a target. ≈ 43 sessions ≈ 36 hours between 2026-09-23 and 2026-11-23.
- Starting point: hiragana ~80% solid, **katakana under 10%** — effectively a script
  to learn from scratch. Kana is the first and largest blocker. (Corrected 2026-09-23;
  see [[learning-records/0002-katakana-is-from-scratch.md]].)
- Owns the Genki I 3rd-edition textbook and workbook PDFs; no tutor, no class.
- Self-taught and self-paced — every exercise must be self-scoring, because there
  is no teacher in the room to mark it.

## Out of scope

- **Handwriting production.** Stroke order is learned only where it disambiguates
  a character you must *read* (シ/ツ, ン/ソ). Writing kanji from memory is not
  the mission; recognising them is.
- Keigo, casual-speech nuance, pitch accent, and Genki II grammar.
- Genki I Lessons 7–12 within the two-month window — mapped in the roadmap for
  months 3–4, deliberately not crammed into this one. See [[learning-records/0001-two-month-scope-reality-check.md]].
- **Lesson 6 (て-form) is now a stretch goal, not a commitment.** The katakana
  correction cost Phase 1 two sessions. Committed scope is Lessons 1–5; Lesson 6
  takes a sixth session and spills into month 3 rather than being compressed.

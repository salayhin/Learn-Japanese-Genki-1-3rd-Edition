# Katakana is a from-scratch script, not a gap to patch

The intake self-assessment of "40–50% katakana" was corrected by the learner on
2026-09-23 to **under 10%** — roughly 42 of 46 characters genuinely unknown.
Hiragana remains at ~80%. This changes the shape of Phase 0 rather than its goal.

**Implications:**

- **Lesson 0001 was mis-sequenced.** It teaches the <span>シ/ツ/ン/ソ</span>
  discrimination, which presupposes knowing the other 42 characters. Discrimination
  is not acquisition — you cannot tell two shapes apart before meeting either. It
  moved from session 1 to session 5, after the full set is known. The lesson content
  is fine; only its position was wrong.
- **Phase 0 grew from 7 sessions to 9**, and hiragana moved to session 1. Hiragana
  first is now load-bearing, not just a quick win: ~20 katakana derive from the same
  kanji as a hiragana twin (か→カ, き→キ, せ→セ, り→リ, へ→ヘ), so solid hiragana
  converts directly into free katakana. That observation became Lesson 0004.
- **The kana gate split into two targets.** Nine sessions cannot take 42 brand-new
  characters to sub-second recognition. The gate is now *hiragana under 1.0 s/char
  and katakana 100% accurate*, with katakana **speed** carried by the daily
  five-minute warm-up through Genki Lessons 1–3 (expected to land late October).
  Holding Genki hostage to katakana speed would cost three weeks and gain nothing.
- **Phase 1 lost two sessions**, so Lessons 1, 5 and 6 drop to five sessions each.
  **Lessons 1–5 are now the committed scope; Lesson 6 is likely, not guaranteed.**
  Since Lesson 6 is the て-form, the standing instruction is: if it needs a sixth
  session it takes one and spills into month 3. Do not compress て-form to hit a date.
  Supersedes the scope framing in [[0001-two-month-scope-reality-check]], which
  assumed six lessons were safe.

**General lesson for this workspace:** the learner's self-assessment was off by a
factor of four in the direction that costs the most. Measure before planning around
a stated level — session 1 opens with a diagnostic drill for exactly this reason,
and the katakana sessions should end with one too.

# The two-month window buys Lessons 1–6, not all twelve

The stated goal was "complete Genki I in 2 months with reading fluency." Against
the stated constraint — 45–50 minutes on weekdays, nothing at weekends — the
window from 2026-09-23 to 2026-11-23 contains **43 sessions ≈ 36 hours**. Genki I
is a full university semester, conventionally 90–120 study hours. Twelve lessons
in 36 hours is ~3 hours per lesson including a kana rebuild, which buys exposure
and no retention. Scope was therefore cut to **kana fluency + Lessons 1–6**, with
Lessons 7–12 mapped onto months 3–4.

**Evidence:** user-stated constraints, 2026-09-23. Kana self-assessment: hiragana
~80%, katakana ~40–50%. Goal set includes JLPT N5/N4, which rewards retention over
coverage and so breaks the tie toward depth.

**Implications:**

- Lessons 1–6 is not an arbitrary stopping point. It ends on the て-form (L6),
  which is the single highest-leverage structure in elementary Japanese and the
  gateway to most of Genki II. Stopping at L5 would end mid-adjective; stopping
  at L7 would mean rushing て-form, the worst possible thing to rush.
- Reading fluency is the *primary* success metric, not a by-product — so the
  読み書き編 half of the textbook (book pp.296+) is treated as core, not optional.
  Most self-learners skip it. This plan does not.
- Genki 読み書き Lessons 1 and 2 *are* hiragana and katakana instruction. The kana
  sprint is therefore inside the book, not a detour from it.
- The weekend gap (2 days, twice per lesson block) is a spacing opportunity, not
  only a loss. Monday sessions are built as retrieval-first by design.
- If the user later reports the pace is too slow, the honest lever is Lesson 7,
  not compressing L1–L6.

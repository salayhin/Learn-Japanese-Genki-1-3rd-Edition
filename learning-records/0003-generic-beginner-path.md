# The plan is now a generic beginner path, not a personalised one

At the learner's request the roadmap was rewritten to assume **zero prior Japanese** and to
use **weeks and weekday names instead of calendar dates**, so that any student can follow it
and so it can be handed to someone else. Supersedes the personalised framing in
[[0001-two-month-scope-reality-check]] and [[0002-katakana-is-from-scratch]], whose
*reasoning* still holds — only the audience changed.

**What changed as a consequence:**

- **Lesson 0002 was rebuilt from scratch.** It previously opened with a gap-finding
  diagnostic on the assumption of ~80% hiragana. A true beginner has no gap to find, so it
  is now five parts teaching all 46 characters row by row, organised around the idea that
  *hiragana is a grid, not an alphabet* — five vowels in a fixed a-i-u-e-o order, then ten
  rows reusing them. The look-alike material survives intact as Part 5.
- **Phase 0 grew to 13 sessions** (weeks 1–3): a full week for hiragana, a full week for
  katakana, then marks, beats and the gate. Learning hiragana from nothing costs a week that
  the earlier plan didn't need to spend.
- **Committed scope dropped from Lessons 1–6 to Lessons 1–5.** 45 sessions ≈ 37 hours; 13 go
  to the writing system, leaving 32. Lesson 6 and the て-form moved into the continuation
  map, flagged there as *the one to protect* — it is the highest-leverage structure in
  elementary Japanese and the worst possible thing to rush.
- **Hiragana-before-katakana became load-bearing rather than merely sensible.** Week 2 Part 1
  hands the learner ~20 katakana for free by pointing at hiragana twins sharing a source
  kanji (か→カ, き→キ, せ→セ, へ→ヘ). That only pays off if week 1 actually stuck, so
  Lesson 0001 now opens by telling the reader to do week 1 first.

**Implications for future sessions:**

- Two lesson pages are still unwritten: **week 2 Friday** (katakana in whole words) and
  **week 3 Wednesday** (the kana gate). Build them when reached.
- `MISSION.md` remains personal to this learner and still records their actual starting
  point. The roadmap no longer does. Keep that split — the mission is the compass for *this*
  person, the roadmap is the path for *anyone*. Don't let one overwrite the other.
- Any future lesson must use week/day labels, never dates, or the path stops being portable.

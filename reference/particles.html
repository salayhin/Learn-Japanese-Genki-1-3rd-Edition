Particles

Every particle Genki teaches up to Lesson 5 on one page, with the rule that catches the most people, に or で, first.

Jump to に or で All particles No particle Practice

A particle comes after the word it marks and tells you what that word is doing in the sentence. English shows this with word order; Japanese shows it with particles. That's why you can move a phrase around in Japanese and still be understood, as long as its particle comes with it (textbook p. 88).

に or で? Ask one question

Both can come after a place, so you can't choose by looking at the place. Look at the verb and ask: does something happen there, or does something arrive at or exist in it?

で

Something happens there

  • Where an action takes place としょかんで べんきょうします Lesson 3 · pp. 88–89
  • Where an event is held, even with あります だいがくで テストが あります Lesson 4 · p. 107
に

Something arrives, exists, or is pinned there

  • Where you go, come or return to がっこうに いきます Lesson 3 · p. 89
  • A fixed point in time にちようびに · 三時に Lesson 3 · pp. 89–90
  • Where something or someone exists こうえんに いぬが います Lesson 4 · p. 107
  • The person you meet, the person you ask ともだちに あいます Lesson 4 · p. 105 · Lesson 5 · p. 131
  • The vehicle you get on バスに のります Lesson 5 · p. 131

Same place, different particle

Each pair keeps the place the same. Only the verb changes, and the verb decides.

がっこうに いきますI go to school. You arrive there.
がっこうで べんきょうしますI study at school. Something happens there.
うちに ねこが いますThere's a cat at home. It exists there.
うちで ばんごはんを たべますI eat dinner at home. Something happens there.
だいがくに としょかんが ありますThere's a library at the university. A thing exists.
だいがくで テストが ありますThere's a test at the university. An event is held.
The trap in the last pair

Both sentences end in あります, so the verb alone doesn't tell you. Ask what the が word is. A thing (a library) exists: に. An event (a test, a party, a class) happens: で. The book makes this point on p. 107.

に or へ?

For where you're going, へ (read e) can replace に: うちへ かえります is fine. へ only means direction, so it can never mark a time: ×にちようびへ. If you're unsure, use に, which works in both cases (p. 89).

The errors that feel right

  • としょかんに べんきょうしますとしょかんで べんきょうしますStudying happens there → で.
  • こうえんで いぬが いますこうえんに いぬが いますExisting isn't an action → に.
  • まいにちに コーヒーを のみますまいにち コーヒーを のみます"Every day" isn't a fixed point → no particle.
  • ともだちを あいましたともだちに あいましたEnglish "meet a friend" has an object, but Japanese uses に.
  • バスを のりましたバスに のりましたYou get onto the bus → に.

Not yet: で also means by or with, as in バスで いきます (I go by bus). That use first appears in Lesson 10 (p. 238). Put it next to バスに のります when you get there, because the two are easy to mix up.

Why も behaves two ways

Lesson 2's rule was "も replaces は". Lesson 4 adds the rest: も replaces は, が and を, but joins onto に and で. So you write わたしも, never ×わたしはも, but you write こうえんにも, and dropping the に loses the meaning.

When there is no particle

Practice

Answer before you look at the options if you can: say the particle out loud first, then tap it. A dash (—) means no particle. A missed question comes back at the end.

The Confusable Kana

The two dozen characters that cause almost all beginner misreadings, and the rules that separate them. Print this one.

The big four: シ ツ ン ソ

These four account for more katakana errors than every other character combined. They are not four separate problems — they are one 2×2 grid, decided by two questions.

Question 1 — how many short strokes?
Two short strokes → it's shi or tsu. One → it's n or so.

Question 2 — which way does the long stroke sweep?
Upward, from bottom-left to top-right → shi or n. Downward, from top-right to bottom-left → tsu or so.

Why the rule is true, not just a trick

Each katakana was carved out of a kanji, and it inherited that kanji's writing direction — which is also the direction of its hiragana cousin. So the sweep is not arbitrary:

  • し → シ — し ends by curving upward to the right. So does シ.
  • つ → ツ — つ is drawn from the top, moving across and down. So is ツ.
  • ん → ン — ん finishes sweeping upward. So does ン.
  • そ → ソ — そ finishes sweeping down to the left. So does ソ.

A secondary cue for when a font makes the sweep hard to judge: on シ and ン the short strokes stack vertically down the left edge. On ツ and ソ they sit side by side along the top.

Katakana look-alikes

The ク family — count the corners

ウ ワ ク タ ケ

u · wa · ku · ta · ke

ウuHas the little hat tick on top. Nothing else here does.
ワwaSame as ウ but bare-headed. Wide, open, round shoulder.
クkuNarrower than ワ; the top stroke cuts in from the left.
タtaク with a slash through it. ku + 1 stroke = ta.
ケkeA tall vertical drops right through the body.

The ノ family — build by adding strokes

ノ メ ヌ ス

no · me · nu · su

ノnoOne stroke. The base of the whole family.
メmeノ crossed by a stroke — an X. Two strokes.
ヌnuメ that grew a flat roof on top.
スsuRoof + one leg, no crossing. ヌ minus the crossbar.

Not characters, but they break reading anyway

MarkWhat it doesExample
ーLong vowel, katakana only. Holds the previous vowel for a second beat.コーヒー kōhī
っ / ッSmall tsu. Not read — it doubles the next consonant and inserts a pause.きって kitte
ゃ ゅ ょSmall y-kana. Fuse with the previous character into one beat, not two.きょう kyō, not ki-yo-u
はRead wa when it is the topic particle.私は watashi wa
へRead e when it is the direction particle.東京へ Tōkyō e
をAlways read o. Only ever used as the object particle.パンを pan o

What makes this lesson tricky

Not the past tense — that's mechanical and you'll have it in a day. It's あります/います, which overturns the particles you spent Lesson 3 learning. Where you learned で for place, this needs に. Where you learned は for the thing, this needs が. Expect that to feel wrong for a week. It isn't — it's a different construction with its own rules.

Day 2

あります and います

Textbook pp. 107–108. Two verbs meaning "there is", and a particle pattern that contradicts your instincts.

(place に)   thing が あります
(place に)   person が います There is / there are …

Which verb you use depends on the thing:

VerbUsed for
あります Non-living things. Objects, buildings, and also abstract things like time or a class.
います People and other living, moving things — animals included.

The split is really animate-and-moving versus everything else, not "alive versus dead" — plants take あります.

The particle flip — the whole difficulty of this lesson

In Lesson 3 you learned で for where an action happens, and you have used は for the thing being talked about since Lesson 1. This construction uses neither:

でplace of action
→
にplace of existence
はtopic
→
がthe thing that exists

The usual order is place first, then the thing — you set the scene, then say what's in it.

あそこに マクドナルドが あります。asoko ni Makudonarudo ga arimasu There's a McDonald's over there.
あそこに りゅうがくせいが います。asoko ni ryuugakusee ga imasu There's an international student over there.

The same verbs mean "have"

テレビが ありません。terebi ga arimasen I don't have a TV.
じかんが ありますか。jikan ga arimasu ka Do you have time?
にほんじんの ともだちが います。nihonjin no tomodachi ga imasu I have a Japanese friend. — a person, so います

And "an event takes place"

かようびに テストが あります。kayoobi ni tesuto ga arimasu There's an exam on Tuesday.
One exception worth knowing

When あります means an event takes place, the place goes back to で — because an event is something that happens rather than something that sits there: あしたは きょうとで おまつりが あります (there's a festival in Kyoto tomorrow). Existence takes に; occurrence takes で.

います is not です

English uses "is" for both, which hides a real distinction. あそこに りゅうがくせいが います — there exists a student over there. メアリーさんは りゅうがくせいです — Mary is a student, an attribute. います/あります are for existence and location; です is for attributes. Never swap them.

Day 3

Describing Where Things Are

Textbook pp. 108–109. Yesterday said that something exists; today says where, relative to something else.

In Lesson 2 you could only answer どこですか with あそこです — "over there". Now you can be precise, and the pattern is just Noun の Noun from Lesson 1 with a location word in the second slot:

X は Y の [location] です。 X is [location] of Y
ぎんこうは としょかんの となりです。ginkoo wa toshokan no tonari desu The bank is next to the library.
かさは テーブルの したです。kasa wa teeburu no shita desu The umbrella is under the table.

"Between" needs two reference points and uses と to join them:

X は Y と Z の あいだ です。 X is between Y and Z
レストランは スーパーと びょういんの あいだです。 resutoran wa suupaa to byooin no aida desu The restaurant is between the supermarket and the hospital.
となり means side by side, same kind

となり is for two things of the same category standing next to each other — two buildings, two people. かばんは つくえの となりです is odd, because a bag and a desk aren't peers. For "beside" in a general sense, use よこ. And そば is another everyday word for "near", alongside ちかく.

Using a location with a verb

If something happens at that spot rather than just sitting there, you're back to で — the Lesson 3 rule, unchanged:

モスバーガーの まえで メアリーさんを まちました。 Mosubaagaa no mae de Mearii san o machimashita I waited for Mary in front of the Mos Burger.

Note まえ often means opposite or across the street from, not literally "in front of the entrance" — and むかい is the more explicit word for that. Similarly うら is used for something round the back and out of sight, where うしろ would be merely "behind".

Check it

Day 4

The Past Tense

Textbook p. 110. The easiest day in the lesson and the one that buys the most.

Two tables, and you have doubled your expressive range. Nothing irregular, nothing to work out — the forms just swap.

です

AffirmativeNegative
Present〜です〜じゃないです
Past〜でした〜じゃなかったです
やましたせんせいは がくせいでした。…gakusee deshita Professor Yamashita was a student.
あれは にほんの えいがじゃなかったです。…ja nakatta desu That was not a Japanese film.

Verbs

AffirmativeNegative
Present〜ます〜ません
Past〜ました〜ませんでした
メアリーさんは くじごろ うちに かえりました。…kaerimashita Mary went home at about nine.
きのう にほんごを べんきょうしませんでした。…benkyooshimasen deshita I didn't study Japanese yesterday.
The hard work was already done

The る/う/irregular machinery from Lesson 3 applies unchanged. Get to 〜ます and the past is simply 〜ました. So かえる → かえります → かえりました. If your verb groups are solid, you learned the past tense in about ninety seconds. If they're not, this is the moment that shows it — go back to Lesson 3, day 2.

Day 5

も, Duration, たくさん, と

Textbook pp. 111–113. Four small points, one of which is genuinely subtle.

1 · も, and what it does to other particles

You met も in Lesson 2 replacing は. It also works when two people do the same thing, or one person does two things. The new and important part is what happens with other particles:

は · が · をreplaced
→
もthe particle disappears
に · で · …kept
→
にも · でもも stacks on top
メアリーさんは かばんも かいました。…kaban mo kaimashita Mary bought a bag too. — を replaced by も
おおさかにも いきました。Oosaka ni mo ikimashita I went to Osaka too. — に kept, も added
カフェでも ほんを よみました。kafe de mo hon o yomimashita He read a book at a café too. — で kept, も added

The logic: は・が・を mark grammatical roles, and も is filling that same slot. に and で carry actual meaning — destination, location — which would be lost if も replaced them. So they survive and も is added.

2 · Duration takes no particle at all

〜じかん  +  verb for N hours — a bare noun, no particle
たけしさんを いちじかん まちました。…ichijikan machimashita I waited for Takeshi for an hour.
にほんごを さんじかんぐらい べんきょうしました。…sanjikan gurai… I studied Japanese for about three hours.
きのう ななじかんはん ねました。…nanajikan han nemashita I slept seven and a half hours last night.

Add ぐらい for "about", 半 for "and a half". If you want both, ぐらい goes last: いちじかんはんぐらい.

Don't confuse this with 〜じ

いちじ is one o'clock — a point in time, and it takes に. いちじかん is one hour — a duration, and it takes nothing. One syllable apart, completely different grammar. ごろ goes with the point; ぐらい goes with the duration.

3 · たくさん sits in either place

Unusually flexible — before the noun, or after the particle. Both are correct:

しゃしんを たくさん とりました。shashin o takusan torimashita I took lots of photos.
たくさん しゃしんを とりました。takusan shashin o torimashita Same meaning, other order.

4 · と — "and", and "with"

にほんごと えいごを はなします。nihongo to eego o hanashimasu I speak Japanese and English.
ソラさんと かんこくに いきます。Sora san to Kankoku ni ikimasu With Sora, I'll go to Korea.
と joins nouns only

You cannot use と to join two verbs or two sentences — "I ate and went home" does not work this way. Connecting actions needs the て-form, which is Lesson 6. This is a very common early error precisely because English "and" does both jobs.

Check it

Seven of them are one system

Look at what happens when you combine them with 曜日. The days of the week are named after the classical planets — moon, fire, water, wood, gold, earth, sun — exactly as the English and Romance names are. You already know all the characters:

月曜日getsuyoobiMonday · moon
火曜日kayoobiTuesday · fire
水曜日suiyoobiWednesday · water
木曜日mokuyoobiThursday · wood
金曜日kinyoobiFriday · gold

日曜日nichiyoobiSunday · sun

Seven kanji, one suffix, and a whole calendar becomes readable. This is the block after which a shop's opening days and a train timetable stop being decoration. Note 日本 too — "sun origin", the origin of the sun. That's what the country's name means.

Day 7

Reading: メアリーさんのしゅうまつ

Textbook pp. 308–311. Your second five-pass passage — and the first with a comparison point.

Mary's weekend, told in the past tense you learned three days ago. Run the same protocol as Lesson 3:

  1. Listen only. Audio on, eyes following, nothing looked up.
  2. Silent read. Mark where you stall; look up only those.
  3. Chorus read. Aloud, along with the audio, keeping up.
  4. Solo read, timed. Write the time down.
  5. Solo read, timed again. Beat pass 4.
Now you have something to compare against

Pull out your Lesson 3 times. This passage is longer and harder than that one, so a similar time is real improvement and a faster one is substantial. The thing to watch is the gap between pass 4 and pass 5 — if it's narrowing across lessons, your decoding is becoming automatic, which is exactly what the whole plan is for.

Describe your own weekend

All past tense, all Lesson 4 grammar:

  1. せんしゅうの しゅうまつ、ばしょに いきました。Last weekend I went to ___.
  2. ひとと いきました。I went with ___. と = "together with".
  3. そこに ものが ありました。There was a ___ there. に for place, が for the thing.
  4. ばしょの となりでした。It was next to ___. Past of です.
  5. すうじじかんぐらい いました。I was there for about ___ hours. No particle on duration.
  6. ものを たくさん たべました。I ate lots of ___.
  7. でも、なには しませんでした。But I didn't do ___. Past negative.

Lesson 4 reference

Grammar and vocabulary, separated — and kept last on purpose. These are lookup tables you return to, not a study day. Genki prints them as sections for the same reason: you will flip back here far more often than you read it through once.

Grammar

1 · あります and います — and the particle flip

pp. 107–108

place に   thing が   あります / います

  • あります for non-living things; います for people and animals — animate and moving, so a plant takes あります.
  • This overturns Lesson 3. There you learned で for place and は for the topic. Existence takes に and が. It will feel wrong for a week because it contradicts what you just learned.
  • The exception inside the exception: when あります means an event takes place, the place reverts to で. Existence takes に; occurrence takes で.
  • います is not です. English “is” hides a split Japanese keeps: existence and location versus attribute.

2 · Saying where something is

pp. 108–109

X は Y の となり です

  • Location words are nouns, so they join with の exactly as in Lesson 1.
  • となり means next to a peer — two shops, two people, things of the same kind. For a bag beside a desk you want よこ.
  • まえ often means opposite, across the street, as well as literally in front.

3 · The past tense

p. 110

たべました  ·  がくせいでした

  • Verbs: ます→ました, ません→ませんでした.
  • です: でした and じゃなかったです.
  • The Lesson 3 verb-group machinery is unchanged — you are swapping one ending for another, nothing more. If this doesn’t fall out in ninety seconds, the problem is the verb groups, not the past tense.

4 · も, duration, たくさん and と

pp. 111–113
  • も replaces は/が/を but stacks on に/で → にも, でも. The reason: は・が・を mark grammatical roles and も fills that slot, while に・で carry meaning that would be lost.
  • Duration takes no particle at all. いちじかん (one hour) versus いちじ (one o’clock) — one syllable apart, completely different grammar. ぐらい goes with durations, ごろ with points in time.
  • と joins nouns only, and separately means “together with.” It cannot join two verbs — that needs the て-form in Lesson 6.

What you can do now

  • Talk about the past — the single biggest jump in range so far.
  • Say that something exists, that you have it, or that an event is happening.
  • Describe where anything is, relative to anything else.
  • Say how long something took, who you did it with, and that you did two things.
  • Read the days of the week, and therefore any calendar or opening-hours sign.

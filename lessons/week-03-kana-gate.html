Phase 0 · Week 3 · Day 3

The Kana Gate

Not a lesson. A measurement. Three weeks of work gets tested today, and the result decides one thing: whether Genki starts tomorrow, and what carries on into your warm-ups when it does.

Rules of engagement

Nothing open. No charts, no notes, no re-reading a lesson "just to warm up" first. A gate you prepare for five minutes before measures your short-term memory, not your reading. If that feels uncomfortable, that discomfort is the instrument working — take the test cold and get a true number.

The standard

Accuracy is the gate. Speed is not.

You need 100% accuracy across all 104 readings in both scripts. You do not need to be fast, and you almost certainly won't be. Three weeks cannot make 92 brand-new shapes automatic — nothing can. Sub-second recognition is built by the five-minute warm-up drill over the following weeks and typically arrives around Genki Lesson 3.

So: record your times, but don't chase them. Today's times are a baseline to beat later, not a pass mark.

Five tests

Work through them in order. Write each result down as you go — the scoresheet below is laid out to fill in, and the pattern across the five is what tells you what to do next. A single number can't do that.

Test 1

Hiragana — all 46

The script you learned first and have had longest. This is the one that should be cleanest, and if it isn't, that outranks everything else below.

Test 2

Katakana — all 46

Newer, and expect it to feel worse. Watch specifically for シ/ツ and ン/ソ — if those four are your only misses, that is a very different result from misses scattered across the set.

Test 3

Voiced and contracted — both scripts mixed

The other 58 readings per script: ゛, ゜, and the small ゃゅょ. Scripts are interleaved deliberately — switching between them is a skill of its own, and real text never sorts itself for you.

Test 4

Beats

The test that decides whether you can read aloud rather than just name characters. Say each word out loud, tap the beats, then answer. Small っ is silent but counted; small ゃゅょ fuse into one beat; ー is a full beat.

Test 5

Mixed-script reading

The closest thing here to real Japanese: hiragana and katakana in the same line, with the particle readings that trip people up — は as wa, へ as e, を as o. Read each line aloud before answering.

Fill this in. If you are reading on screen, write the numbers somewhere you'll still have them in six weeks — the times are worth beating later, and the misses are what your warm-ups should contain.

What your result means

Find the line that matches. Only one of these stops you.

All five at standard

You're through. Genki Lesson 1 starts tomorrow. Keep the five-minute kana drill in every warm-up — it's now about speed, not accuracy, and it runs until roughly Genki Lesson 3.

Tests 1–3 clean, but 4 or 5 below standard

Characters are solid; the timing rules aren't. Re-do week 3, day 2 as tomorrow's warm-up, then start Genki. This does not hold you back — beats keep improving through the dialogues, which come with audio.

One script has 1–4 misses, clustered

Typically シ/ツ/ン/ソ, or ね/れ/わ. A cluster is a rule you haven't finished installing, not a memory failure. Re-read the relevant part — katakana day 4 or hiragana day 5 — and put exactly those characters in tomorrow's warm-up. Start Genki anyway.

One script has 5+ misses, scattered

This one is worth a day. Scattered misses mean the set didn't consolidate, and no rule will fix it — it needs retrieval. Repeat that script's consolidation day (hiragana day 5 or katakana day 3) tomorrow, re-run this gate the day after, and let Genki start two days late. Two days is a cheap price; carrying a broken script into nine weeks of reading is not.

Everything accurate but very slow

That is a pass. Slow is the expected state at three weeks and is not a reason to delay anything. Speed is a warm-up problem, and the warm-up is already in the plan.

A word about the honest version of this

The temptation is to re-run a test until the number looks good and record that one. Every re-run is easier than the last, because you have just seen the answers — so the second score measures something different from the first, and only the first is the one worth keeping. Record your first attempt. Re-running afterwards as practice is fine and useful; just don't overwrite the measurement with it.

What comes next

Tomorrow is Genki Lesson 1 — あたらしいともだち, textbook pp. 36–55, with greetings and numbers first at pp. 30–35. It's the first day of the six-day rotation described in the path: vocab and dialogue, two days of grammar, a workbook day, a reading day, then retrieval.

Three things worth doing before you close this page:

  • Check OTO Navi is installed and the audio downloads. From tomorrow every lesson opens with listening, and the day-5 reading protocol depends on it entirely.
  • Put the kana charts somewhere you can glance at them — not to study, to check. You'll want them during vocabulary lists for a few weeks yet.
  • Write your miss-list on a sticky note. That short list, not the whole syllabary, is what the five-minute warm-up drill should contain from tomorrow.

You've finished the writing system. Both scripts, all 104 readings each, the voicing marks, and the timing rules — everything except kanji, which don't start until Genki Lesson 3. Three weeks ago none of this page would have been readable. That is the whole point of measuring: so you can see it.
